A. Executive Diagnosis of the Original Document

What the original got right

The original document correctly identified the regulatory gap (PROGA §2(1)(h) social game safe harbour), the B2B data thesis over user monetization, the finance-first market scope, the phone OTP auth strategy for India, the SSR-first performance target, and the importance of calibration as an anti-spam mechanism. These survive into v3.

What was wrong

LMSR is the wrong core mechanism. LMSR is a market maker designed to create price discovery through virtual trading. For a forecasting intelligence platform, it introduces unnecessary complexity, creates synthetic price signals that do not directly represent beliefs, encourages strategic trading behaviour over sincere belief expression, and makes the underlying dataset scientifically noisier. The entire virtual balance, P&L, and position-trading architecture should be removed.

calibrationScore = 1 - Brier is both terminologically wrong and mathematically misleading. Brier score decomposes into reliability (calibration), resolution, and uncertainty. Calling 1 - Brier a calibration score conflates distinct quantities. A random predictor can produce a superficially attractive number under this transform.

No blind elicitation. The original shows crowd probability before a user predicts. This causes anchoring and herding. The fundamental asset of the platform, independent forecasts, is damaged at collection.

The business model framing was inverted. The correct framing is a forecasting network whose consumer product generates a longitudinal skill ledger that becomes intelligence infrastructure. The commercial product is calibrated, attributable, historically verified probability intelligence.

Single-stock markets are excluded from launch. Individual security price predictions create potential SEBI Research Analyst exposure and require legal review before reintroduction.

B. Product Thesis

The central reframe

The old product was a prediction market. A prediction market creates synthetic price signals through virtual positions. The new product is a forecasting network. A forecasting network collects direct probability beliefs from humans, scores them against outcomes, builds a longitudinal skill record, and aggregates calibrated signals into probability intelligence.

A prediction market's product is a price. A forecasting network's product is a calibrated, attributed, historically verified probability with provenance, skill weighting, uncertainty bounds, and a track record.

Forecasters (supply)
    → submit blind probabilities
    → forecasting ledger (append-only)
    → resolution engine (outcome recorded)
    → scoring engine (Brier Skill Score per forecast)
    → reputation engine (domain skill estimates with uncertainty)
    → aggregation engine (skill-weighted, extremized)
    → probability intelligence (public product + B2B API + research)

The consumer product is the mechanism for building the network. The underlying asset is the longitudinal forecasting record. The commercial product is probabilistic intelligence derived from it.

C. Core Mechanism: Blind Direct Probability Elicitation

Why not LMSR

LMSR was designed to create liquid prediction markets with no counterparty. For a forecasting intelligence platform it introduces strategic behaviour, creates prices rather than direct beliefs, generates a noisier dataset, and requires a complex virtual economy. It is removed entirely from v3.

Direct probability elicitation is simpler and produces a cleaner scientific dataset. Users see a question, but not the current crowd probability, other forecasts, or leaderboard position. They submit 1–99%. Only after submission does the system reveal the aggregate and their position relative to the crowd.

Forecast update policy

Every update is a new immutable forecast event with timestamp, probability, forecast version, visibility state, crowd state at submission, and resolution state. The first blind forecast and the latest forecast at close are both scored.

Experiment framework

Future controlled conditions can compare Blind, Visible-consensus, and Visible-leaderboard designs. Calibration and accuracy differences are proprietary research material and should be measured against the blind control.

D. Scoring Methodology

Correct framework

Primary scoring rule: Brier Score

BS(p, o) = (p − o)²

p is stated probability in [0,1], o is outcome 0 or 1. Lower is better.

Skill measurement: Brier Skill Score

BSS = 1 − (BS_forecaster / BS_reference)

BSS > 0 means the forecaster beats the reference. BSS = 1 is perfect. BSS < 0 means worse than the reference. This is the main profile skill metric.

Reference baselines

BaselineUse
Historical base ratePreferred first reference where comparable history exists.
50/50 naiveFallback when historical data is unavailable.
Equal-weight crowdSecondary comparison after enough forecasts exist.
Professional surveyMacro events where published survey data exists.

Murphy decomposition

Internally decompose Brier score into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty. Calibration is a specific property of forecast reliability and should not be conflated with total Brier performance.

Logarithmic score

LS(p, o) = o·log(p) + (1−o)·log(1−p)

Use internally to flag overconfident forecasts. Do not expose it as the primary user metric.

Uncertainty in skill estimates

Use Bayesian shrinkage toward a population prior, display confidence intervals, require at least 20 resolved forecasts for domain ranking, and never treat a tiny sample as authoritative.

F. Aggregation Engine

Stage 1: Equal-weight mean

P_agg = (1/N) · Σ p_i

Launch with the transparent mean. Use it until sufficient skill history exists.

Stage 2: BSS-weighted mean

w_i = max(0, BSS_i_domain) · f(n_i)
P_agg = Σ(w_i · p_i) / Σ(w_i)

Use domain-specific skill with sample-size shrinkage. Do not hard-code arbitrary weighting parameters.

Stage 3: Extremization

P_extremized = P_agg^α / (P_agg^α + (1−P_agg)^α)

Estimate α from Laplace's own resolved corpus using out-of-sample validation. Do not copy a literature value blindly.

Do not add recency decay, correlation adjustment, hierarchical models, or horizon-specific weighting until empirical evidence demonstrates incremental benefit.

O. Consumer Product (MVP)

The simplest thing that proves the network

The MVP should do one thing excellently: take a serious person's probability estimate on a well-defined financial question, score it against the outcome, and build their verifiable record.

2. See questions open for forecasting
3. Open question and read full specification
4. Submit blind probability from 1%–99%
5. Reveal crowd aggregate after submission

W. 90-Day Experiment

The core business question

Will serious people voluntarily submit calibrated probabilities on Indian financial events, return to update them, and create a dataset with predictive value above simple baselines, sufficient to create professional interest?

A small number of resolved questions is a business-validation signal, not proof of general forecasting superiority. Report effect sizes and uncertainty honestly.

Experiment design

Target forecasters: 30–50 recruited directly
Market count: 30 finance-only questions
Elicitation: blind
Resolution: within 48h of outcome availability

Final Decision Framework

Recommendation
VALIDATE FIRST

Do not abandon, but do not build at full speed. Build the minimum necessary to run the 90-day experiment: blind elicitation, proper scoring, 30 questions, recruited forecasters, rigorous resolution, and a measurable scorecard. The experiment should determine whether there is a real forecasting network and a commercially credible data asset.

Build the ledger. Recruit the forecasters. Resolve the questions. Measure the skill. If the experiment succeeds, build. If it fails to attract qualified forecasters or produces no useful signal, redesign or stop.

Founder Questions

Questions that still need answers

  • 1. Do you have existing relationships with economists, finance students, or research associates who can become the first 10 forecasters?
  • 2. Who is your co-founder Ronak, what is his background, and what does he own?
  • 3. How much time per week can each founder commit to question writing and resolution?
  • 4. Do you have a CA and startup counsel contact?
  • 5. Has any financial journalist or publisher agreed to test the widget?
  • 6. Are you willing to operate for 12 months before depending on meaningful revenue?

Uncomfortable questions

  • What if Indian forecasters are not calibrated? If the cohort is no better than random or baseline, the commercial thesis fails.
  • What if B2B customers already have sufficient consensus data? The product must outperform on speed, coverage, cost, or accuracy.
  • What if reputation is not enough to retain forecasters? Decide in advance what incentives can be introduced without creating regulatory exposure.
  • Who resolves ambiguous outcomes? Resolution rules are part of the core product and must be documented.
  • What happens if both real-money optionality and B2B revenue fail? The answer must be a deliberate capital-allocation decision, not accidental drift.

Final answer

If operating with limited capital, build the smallest version that tests the forecasting-network thesis. In the next 30 days, incorporate, define high-quality questions, build the blind submission flow, recruit the first forecasters personally, resolve the first question quickly, and score it.

Abandonment trigger: after 90 days, with 30+ participating forecasters and 8+ resolved questions, the equal-weight crowd has Brier Skill Score ≤ 0 on every resolved question and there is no evidence of professional demand. At that point the underlying value proposition has not been demonstrated.
